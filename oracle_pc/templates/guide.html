READING THE NUMBERS

Every element of the dashboard, what it means, and how to act on it.

1 · Cycle regime (equity-implied)

A label for where the stock market thinks the memory cycle is — computed from momentum and drawdown of an equal-weight basket of memory-maker shares (Micron, SK hynix, SanDisk, Western Digital, Seagate, Samsung). Equities historically lead physical component prices by ~2–3 quarters.

Rule-based in v0 (deliberately transparent). HMM + learned classifiers land in v1/v2 — see under the hood.

2 · Consumer advice (BUY / WAIT / NEUTRAL)

A vote count, not magic:

  • +1 WAIT if regime ∈ {expansion, maturing, peak_risk}
  • +1 WAIT if Korea export YoY is decelerating (kr_d10_accel < 0)
  • +1 WAIT if NAND spot is flat/down over 7 days (stalling = pre-relief)
  • BUY overrides everything when regime = trough_recovery
  • WAIT needs ≥2 votes; otherwise NEUTRAL with reasons shown

3 · Proxy direction model

P(memory equities up next 5d) is a logistic regression trained on two years of daily equity features. It exists so the scoring machinery works from day one — the real targets (physical spot direction) need ~60 days of collected prices before they can be trained on.

  • Brier score: mean squared error of probability forecasts on yes/no outcomes. Guessing 50% forever scores ~0.25. Lower = better. The dashboard shows test-set Brier so you can judge it yourself.
  • majority baseline acc: accuracy of just always guessing the more common outcome. A model barely above this is telling you it knows almost nothing — which is honest.

6 · Prediction ledger & scoreboard

Every run writes its forecasts to an append-only ledger with a hash of the inputs used. When a forecast's horizon elapses, the realized outcome is joined back and both the model and the constant baseline get scored:

  • p_const = always 0.50. It is the enemy. If a model row can't beat it out-of-sample, the scoreboard exposes that permanently.
  • The TrendForce press archive lets GlutClock eventually score the industry's own paid forecasters against reality too — their free releases are captured verbatim each day.
Calibration beats confidence: a forecast of "70%" should come true about 70% of the time. That is the standard the scoreboard is building toward as history accumulates.
