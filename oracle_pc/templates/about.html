WHAT IS GLUTCLOCK?

A small automated research desk for one question: when do RAM and SSDs get cheap again?

The short version

RAM and SSD prices are not set by stores — they are set by a tiny club of factories (Samsung, SK hynix, Micron and a few NAND makers) whose products swing through brutal multi-year cycles: shortage → price explosion → everyone builds new fabs at once → glut → crash. Since 2025 the AI boom has been eating the world's memory supply, and consumer prices roughly doubled. Buying a PC became a timing decision.

GlutClock is a program that wakes up twice a day, reads the same public signals professional analysts watch — spot-chip prices, memory-maker stocks, Korean customs export data, industry forecast press releases — compresses them into a handful of gauges, and renders a verdict: where are we in the cycle, and should you buy components now or wait?

It also writes down a prediction every day and later scores itself against those predictions, publicly, against dumb baselines. If its models stop beating a coin flip, the dashboard says so.

Why memory prices swing so violently

  • An oligopoly with fat swings. Three companies sell ~95% of the world's DRAM. When they all misjudge demand in the same direction, there is no market force to soften the landing.
  • Fabs take years. Announcing a fab is instant; producing usable chips takes 2–4 years. Supply arrives precisely when the demand that justified it has faded.
  • HBM cannibalization. AI accelerator memory (HBM) consumes roughly 3× the wafer capacity per bit of normal DRAM. Every GPU sold silently deletes consumer DRAM supply.
  • Panic amplifies both directions. Buyers double-order during shortages (making them look worse) and cancel during gluts (making them worse).
shortage → panic buying → PEAK → demand destruction
        → new fabs tool in (18–30 mo lag) → GLUT (−50%…−80%)
        → capex cuts → trough → recovery → …repeat

Where we are right now (context at deployment, Aug 2026)

  • Contract DRAM rose ~60% in a single quarter; NAND wafer spot prices stopped rising in July 2026.
  • HBM is sold out through 2026; suppliers say conventional memory stays tight until new fabs land in late 2027–2028.
  • Memory equities crashed 20–47% from June highs despite record earnings — markets price the turn before the shelf price confirms it.
  • PC shipments fell ~11% in 2026; entry-level laptops are disappearing because memory is now ~23% of the bill of materials.
Translation: expensive-but-stabilizing is the base case into 2027, with genuine glut risk concentrated in 2028+ — earlier if AI spending cracks, later if it doesn't. The dashboard's job is to notice the inflection early, not to guess it once.

What GlutClock is not

  • Not investment advice. It predicts commodity directions with tiny sample sizes; it will be wrong.
  • Not a trading system. There is no execution, no broker, no latency game.
  • Not clairvoyant. Physical prices lag equity signals by quarters; "regime = contraction" does not mean RAM is cheap tomorrow.
  • Not finished. The learned models are cold-starting and say so on the dashboard.
